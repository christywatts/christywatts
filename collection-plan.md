# Collection Plan: Bright Africa, Seen From Above

A painting collection that combines **Lukas Leibe** (top-down aerial view, crackle-textured ground, one small realistic figure with a long cast shadow) with **Werner Bronkhorst** (heavy palette-knife impasto, bold color bands and gradients, shaped canvases, tiny figures in a big field, witty titles). The subjects come from specific African places, painted in saturated color.

---

## The combined style in one sentence

A big field of color in one or two hues, thick enough to touch, seen from directly above, with **one small, precisely painted moment** in it and a shadow long enough to tell you the time of day.

| Element | From Leibe | From Bronkhorst | Your version |
|---|---|---|---|
| Viewpoint | Straight down, drone view | Mostly straight down | Always straight down. That's the signature |
| Ground | Crackle paste, dry cracked earth/ice | Knife-swept impasto ridges | **Crackle for land, knife sweeps for water and sky** |
| Color | Muted teal, bone, one red accent | Clean saturated bands and gradients | Saturated. 1 dominant hue + 1 accent, no more |
| Figure | Painted, realistic, long shadow | Tiny (often a real miniature figurine) | Painted at 2–5 cm, sharp edges, long shadow |
| Format | Rectangle, raw edges | Ovals, semicircles, diptychs | Mix: rectangles for land, ovals/arches for sky and water |
| Titles | Plain | Puns ("High Horses", "Head in the Clouds") | Short wordplay that rewards a second look |

**Rule of thumb:** the figure should take up under 3% of the canvas. Leaving the field empty is what makes it work.

---

## The palette

Bright, but grounded in real places, not generic "African" clichés.

- **Namib apricot**: cadmium orange + raw sienna + white
- **Natron pink**: quinacridone rose + titanium white + a touch of cad red
- **Retba rose**: magenta-pink, warmer than Natron
- **Kente gold**: cad yellow medium + yellow ochre
- **Indian Ocean turquoise**: phthalo turquoise + white (the Leibe teal, pushed brighter)
- **Dallol sulfur**: lemon yellow + phthalo green + white
- **Shuka red**: pyrrole red (the Maasai cloth red; use it as your accent across the series)
- **Ndebele set**: ultramarine, cad yellow, pyrrole red, emerald, black outlines

Use **Shuka red** as the one recurring accent across all pieces (a cloth, a boat, a bus, a figure). It ties the collection together the way Leibe's red drips do.

## The 10 pieces

### 1. Red on White, Amboseli
- **Scene:** A single Maasai figure in a red shuka walks across a bleached, cracked salt pan. Long blue-violet shadow pointing upper-left.
- **Ground:** Heavy crackle medium over white modeling paste; faint warm grey in the cracks.
- **Format:** 50 × 70 cm rectangle, portrait.
- **Why it's first:** one figure, one ground, one accent. It teaches you the whole method.

### 2. Pink Slip, Lake Natron
- **Scene:** A loose diagonal line of ~12 flamingos crossing pink soda crust; their shadows are long, thin and comma-shaped.
- **Ground:** Crackle paste tinted Natron pink, with darker rose glazed into the cracks. Leave one patch of red-brick crust (Natron's real algae color).
- **Format:** 60 × 60 cm square.

### 3. Salt of the Earth, Lac Retba, Senegal
- **Scene:** Three women carrying white salt basins on their heads, wading through rose-colored water. White salt mounds in a row along one edge.
- **Ground:** Knife-swept impasto in Retba rose, with horizontal ridges like Bronkhorst's bands. Salt mounds are thick white paste, built up 1 cm.
- **Format:** Horizontal arch (semicircle) canvas.

### 4. Dye Hard, Fez
- **Scene:** Seen from above: a grid of round dye wells (saffron, poppy red, indigo, henna green, white lime). Two tiny workers stand waist-deep in different colors.
- **Ground:** Each well is a thick impasto disc; the stone between them is fine crackle in pale ochre.
- **Format:** 80 × 80 cm square. The most graphic, high-color piece in the set.

### 5. Prime Pirogues, Elmina, Ghana
- **Scene:** Five long painted fishing canoes, side by side, pulled up where the surf meets the sand. Each carries painted stripes and flags. One fisherman mends a net.
- **Ground:** Top half is turquoise surf in knife sweeps with white foam ridges; bottom half is gold sand crackle.
- **Format:** Tall diptych (two 30 × 90 cm panels), split down the middle of the boats.

### 6. Out of Line, Lagos
- **Scene:** One yellow danfo minibus on a thick black road that snakes across a bright cobalt field (a direct nod to Bronkhorst's "Prime Positions").
- **Ground:** Black road is thick glossy impasto; cobalt field is flat knife-swept bands getting lighter toward the top.
- **Format:** Triptych of 3 small panels (25 × 35 cm) with the road crossing all three.

### 7. Wall Flower, Ndebele
- **Scene:** Front-on view, the one break from top-down: a huge geometric Ndebele mural fills the canvas. A tiny woman on a ladder paints the last unfinished shape.
- **Ground:** Smooth, crisp hard-edged color blocks with black outlines; a slight lime-wash texture.
- **Format:** 70 × 100 cm portrait.

### 8. Edge Case, Victoria Falls
- **Scene:** A single swimmer lies at the lip of the falls; white spray explodes off the lower edge of the canvas.
- **Ground:** Upper 2/3 is calm emerald-turquoise with gentle sweeps; lower 1/3 is chaotic thick white impasto spray, built up high.
- **Format:** Vertical oval (Bronkhorst "Himmelblau" shape).

### 9. Long Way Round, Okavango
- **Scene:** An elephant mother and calf crossing a turquoise channel, wake rippling behind them. Lily pads as tiny green dots.
- **Ground:** Turquoise knife ridges curving around the elephants like a wake; reed edges in thick ochre strokes along one side.
- **Format:** 60 × 90 cm portrait.

### 10. Sulfur Sunday, Dallol, Ethiopia
- **Scene:** One person in white walks across Dallol's acid-yellow and green crust. This is the most surreal-looking real place on the list.
- **Ground:** Crackle paste tinted sulfur yellow; small mounded "salt chimneys" in green and orange impasto.
- **Format:** 50 × 50 cm square.

**Optional closer: Golden Hour, Namib.** A thin caravan of three figures on a dune ridge, on an oval canvas graded from deep red at the bottom to apricot and pale gold at the top (a nod to Bronkhorst's "Abendrot", but in Namib colors).

## Process (per painting)

1. **Prime and plan.** Gesso, then pencil only the figure and shadow position. Put the figure on a rule-of-thirds point, never dead center.
2. **Build the ground.**
   - *Land:* thin layer of modeling paste → crackle medium (thicker gives bigger cracks) → let it crack overnight → tinted top coat.
   - *Water and sky:* heavy-body acrylic mixed 1:1 with gel medium, then long single-direction strokes with a large palette knife. Don't go back over a stroke.
3. **Glaze the cracks.** Thin a darker version of the base color, wipe it over, then wipe it off the surface so it stays only in the cracks.
4. **Paint the shadow first,** before the figure, with a transparent cool glaze (ultramarine + a little of the ground color). It should be 2–4× longer than the figure.
5. **Paint the figure** using 000–0 brushes, opaque paint, and hard edges. Detail goes here and nowhere else.
6. **Varnish.** Use satin overall; gloss only on water and the figure, so they catch the light.

### Materials
- Heavy-body acrylics (the palette above)
- Modeling paste and crackle paste (e.g. Golden, Liquitex, or a local equivalent)
- Gel medium (heavy) for impasto volume
- Palette knives: one large straight (10 cm+), one small trowel
- Detail brushes: 000, 00, 0
- Optional: HO-scale (1:87) figurines if you want to try Bronkhorst's real-miniature approach on one piece

---

## Keeping it yours

- Use the *language* of both artists, not their compositions. Leibe already has a dhow-on-turquoise piece and polar bears on crackle. Bronkhorst already has the snaking road and the oval sunsets. Your subjects, palette and titles are where the work becomes yours.
- The recurring **Shuka red accent** and **real, named places** are your signature. Keep every title tied to its place.
- Film every piece. Both artists grew their audience through process reels (knife sweeps and the "tiny figure reveal" perform best). A slow pull-back from the figure to the full canvas works well as a reel.

## Suggested order

1 → 2 → 10 (crackle skills) → 8 → 9 (impasto water) → 3 → 5 → 4 (multi-figure and color-heavy) → 6 → 7 (graphic pieces).

## Series 2: The Big Five, East Africa (Bronkhorst style, no cracks)

Thick palette-knife bands of near-pure color, shaped canvases, white shadow-box frames, and a figure so small you have to step closer. Design studies: `designs/bronkhorst-style/` (regenerate with `python3 designs/src/build_wb.py && node designs/src/render.js wb`).

| # | Title | Animal | Place | Surface | Format |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Seeing Red** | Elephant | Tsavo, Kenya | Arched knife bands, cream → orange → deep oxblood | Vertical oval (pill) |
| 2 | **Pride of Place** | Lion, resting | Serengeti, Tanzania | Horizontal gold bands, pale butter → amber | Semicircle |
| 3 | **Spot On** | Leopard on a canopy dab | Samburu, Kenya | Sand bands under thick green knife dabs (acacia canopy from above) | Square, shadow box |
| 4 | **Herd Mentality** | 9 buffalo in a rising line | Ngorongoro Crater, Tanzania | Horizontal pink bands, blush → flamingo | Long landscape panel, shadow box |
| 5 | **Plus One** | Black rhino (left), calf (right) | Amboseli, Kenya | Cream diagonal sweeps, one ridge per stroke | Diptych, two squares |

How to paint them:
- **Paint body:** heavy-body acrylic mixed about 1:1 with heavy gel (or modeling paste for the thickest ridges). Mix every band's color in advance, about 1 cup per band on a 60 cm canvas.
- **One pass per band:** load a wide knife (10–15 cm), lay it flat, and pull across in one stroke, lifting at the end so the paint piles into a ridge. Each new band overlaps the last one's edge. Never rework a stroke; the ridges are the design.
- **Arcs (oval):** pivot the knife from your elbow so each band curves. Work from the top down, lightest to darkest.
- **Dabs (leopard):** press a loaded trowel knife down and twist slightly; don't drag. Leave sand showing between dabs.
- **Figures:** let the paint cure 2–3 days, then either paint the animal at 1.5–3 cm with a 000 brush, or glue on a hand-painted 1:87 (HO-scale) figurine, which is Bronkhorst's actual method. Add a small, soft cast shadow with a thin glaze.
- **Frames:** white floater or shadow-box frames, with a 3–5 cm gap around the canvas. Shaped canvases (oval, semicircle) hang unframed.
